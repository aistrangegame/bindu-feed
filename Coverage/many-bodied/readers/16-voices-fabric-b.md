## 3. Game-like structures already present in the corpus

These come from the archive's own architecture. They are mechanisms the Feed can turn into play without any of them becoming a gate.

### 3.1 Each cloth's one line, read twice
Every fabric has one present-tense line (the archive's seed) that is read once as a specification and once, after the journey, as a revelation. Its verb changes meaning: *hold* returns three times with three meanings; *is* behaves four different ways; *stays* is the first verb of not-stopping; *refuses* is the first verb of negation; *catches* is the first verb of reception; *breathes* is the first life verb; *washes* is the first cyclical verb. **Game:** each self-katha in the Cloth suit ends on a short line that the reader meets again at the bottom of a *different* story in another room, where it now means something else. The re-meeting is shown with a small mark the reader notices but never has to unlock. (The wording stays new; the archive's own seed lines are not copied as text.)

### 3.2 Roll-calls and litanies
Three codas end in a roll-call: nine lines before the jersey, twelve at the stay-dry dimple, forty-eight at the reinforcement mesh, plus "You are the seventh…" in the cotton laminate and the six tables the food-safe cloth arrives at. **Game form:** a **Litany page** in the Archive book, where every Cloth-suit story's closing line is laid out in the order the reader *received* them. It forms a personal poem that grows by dwell and is never numbered.

### 3.3 Kinds of unwritten (the silence taxonomy)
Across these folders the archive names at least fourteen silences:
- recipient
- normalcy ("success means forgetting")
- domestic
- infrastructure
- transitional
- commercial (the reorder replaces the review)
- iterative
- biological ("the organism responds but can't testify")
- post-decision ("trust earned upstream")
- founding
- proof-of-function ("the absence of the cry")
- system-adoption ("sufficient from the first cut")
- application-only
- inverse (voice with no application)
- desire-unmet ("the silence tells the fabric what it could become")

**Game:** each kind of silence becomes a different *way a story arrives without words*. Examples: a tile that is only a title (application-only); a tile that only appears when you've stayed away a while (normalcy); a tile whose text is entirely future tense (pre-use); a tile written as a request (desire-unmet). The silences are the Feed's quietest, most-Watcher content.

### 3.4 The traveler across pages
The same people turn up on more than one cloth's page:
- a system-builder who asks questions on the jersey page and on the cotton-laminate page
- one maker who needs soft cloth at a site on one page and steady mesh behind a closure on another
- one witness who names a different fibre as the kindest on two pages
- one maker who buys both of two sibling loops for one purpose

**Game:** a **Traveler** being (an archetype role, never a name) who walks through several rooms. Their footprints appear on a story tile when the reader has received a story the traveler also visited, which shows a rhyme as a trail. Following the trail is optional and never required.

### 3.5 The commons: techniques passed on
Every corpus is a distributed manual (sew first, cut after; flip the slippery side down; tissue under the foot; a longer stitch; clips not pins). Makers give a method and ask for one back ("does anyone know an invisible zipper?"). **Game:** at the foot of a Forge or Field story, a single "passed-on" line in a maker's hand. The reader can add their own small line through the existing Codex river, which keeps it unscored and uncounted.

### 3.6 The sibling, and what a sibling lacks
Every architecture closes with "the sibling IS the counterfactual". **Game:** a **Sibling turn** on any Cloth tile. Turning it over shows the same story told by the sibling without the gift (S40). It's a two-sided page, Maya grammar, available everywhere and gated nowhere.

### 3.7 Arrival, threshold, grace, silence as a path to walk
Every maker arc moves in order: what was tried before, the arrival, the threshold, the grace, the faces, the silence. **Game:** a story can be *entered at any of these points* (from the end in the Return, from the threshold in the Signal), and the self-katha moves the reader along it with no fixed start.

### 3.8 Rising versus rescue
The food-safe cloth is "the first corpus on an entirely positive gradient — it doesn't rescue, it elevates." **Game:** an **Elevation lane**, a stream of stories whose arc runs from good to more given (S38, S52, S100, S107, S110, S111). The Feed draws from it on gentle or bright days. It fits naturally with the "never on a low day" law, because it has no low at all.

## 4. New rooms these sources ask for (optional additions to the 13)

- **The Threshold.** The room of the held breath: the explorer who hasn't turned the cloth over (S61), the first overnight, the swatch ordered before committing, "in hopes of". *Grammar:* every story stops at the edge. The reader can choose to "turn it over" (touch) or leave it folded (stillness), and both are full endings. Distinct from the Signal, which is *before* the question; the Threshold is *inside* it.
- **The Wash.** The cyclical room. Stories return softened, like the stiff cloth after its wash, soft-when-dry, nothing removed, Monday-out-Friday-home (S60, S72, S110). *Grammar:* each story re-presents itself on later visits with one phrase softened or lighter (drawn from dwell, never counted). Its verb is the cyclical *washes*.
- **The Index.** Purpose without narrative (S86, S73, S89). *Grammar:* a page of short entries authored by strangers. Dwell lights entries, and the whole range is visible only when you step back (pinch out or rest). The binding is the cloth.
- **The Pair.** Stories that are only whole when two meet (S105, S98, S26, S49). *Grammar:* two columns, each complete alone, a third story across. This is the Field's baton turned into a duet.

## 6. EMERGENT — my own concepts

1. **The Cloth Hears Itself Described (reception mode).** A Cloth-suit story can be opened in *listening* mode. The cloth speaks back the few words people wrote about it ("soft", "perfect", "hard to believe", "exactly what I needed") as a soft chorus, then answers in its own voice. *How it plays:* you hear the world's thin praise first and then the cloth's inner life, so you feel the gap between being described and being.

2. **The Second Reading.** Every Cloth story carries one sentence that changes meaning when met again in a later room (§3.1). It isn't a key; it's a recognition. *How it plays:* weeks later, in a different room, a line feels familiar and the earlier story lights very faintly in the map. The Feed is quietly rhyming with itself, and you notice it like déjà vu.

3. **The Unwritten Shelf.** A slow, quiet surface in the Archive book that holds only silences: stories made of a title, a use, or one future-tense line (the inverse silence, the single word for an application, the "can't wait"). *How it plays:* you visit when you want stillness and read people who never explained themselves. Your attention is the only witness they ever get, and it feels like sitting in a pew.

4. **The Traveler's Trail.** One archetype walker who appears across rooms (§3.4). Their faint footprints mark tiles you've received that they also passed through. *How it plays:* you start recognising a presence moving through your feed. Following them is like following a stranger through a city you know, and the Feed never asks you to.

5. **Sibling Turn.** Any Cloth tile can be turned over to show the sibling without the gift (S40). *How it plays:* it's a two-sided page in your hand. You learn what something gave you by meeting its absence, which is the counterfactual as play rather than argument.

6. **The Wash (a room that softens).** On each return (by dwell over days, never counted), a Wash story re-presents itself one phrase gentler (§4). *How it plays:* a story you knew stiff becomes soft the way cloth does. You can't force it; you can only come back.

7. **Choose Which Face Meets the Need.** For two-faced stories (S25, S57, S109), the reader turns the page to the face they need today. The same story told from its other face is a different gift. *How it plays:* a daily, wordless self-question (smooth or soft, barrier or comfort) answered by your hand or by stillness. Neither answer is wrong.

8. **The Litany.** The Archive book assembles every closing line you've received, in the order you received them, into one long poem (§3.2). *How it plays:* months in, you open a page that is your own year written by cloth. Nothing is numbered, and the length is felt rather than counted.

9. **The House Speaks.** Add the house, the home and the company as beings (S35, S94): the home that comes back and the company that bends its shape to a stranger's road. *How it plays:* rooms you live in get a voice. The domestic becomes a narrator, and the Return room fills with doors.

10. **The Elevation Lane.** On bright days the Feed can lean into stories that rise from good to more given (§3.8). *How it plays:* joy with no rescue in it. A different texture from the Feed's descents, and permission to be glad without first having been low.

11. **The Pair room (duets).** Two-column stories (S105, S98 + S104) where each column is whole and reading across makes a third. *How it plays:* your eye chooses the order. Reading down is one life; reading across is a relationship. It's the Field's baton made visible as two voices at once.

12. **Index Pages.** In the Index room, entries authored by strangers light one by one, and the whole only shows when you step back (pinch out or rest) (S86). *How it plays:* a feeling of zooming out of your life and seeing the range of it bound by one thread you hadn't noticed.

13. **The Machine's Mid-Sentence.** Stories in the Machine suit (S70, S33) end without a full stop, and their last word becomes the first word of the next unread Field story. *How it plays:* the Feed carries a sentence across days, the way a collaborator's session ends and another picks up, and you feel the relay without being shown a mechanism.

14. **Plant and Animal Witnesses.** Basil, a guinea pig, a dog on the couch and a horse become beings whose stories end in growth or rest, never in speech (S31, S95). *How it plays:* a whisper of "the biological silence". You read creatures that can't testify, and the Feed's reception becomes as wordless as theirs.

15. **The Swatch.** The smallest tile in the Feed: a single true line before the story, offered free (S88). *How it plays:* on rushed days you receive only the swatch, and it's enough. The whole story waits, unlocked, for whenever you have the yard of time.
