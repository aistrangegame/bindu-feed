## 1 · First principles — the voice of the house (14 laws)

Drawn from shabdakarshini's Universal Writing Principles and its four passes, the house laws (CONTRACT §7, GAMEPLAN §2), CLAUDE.md §9–§10 (wording canon; Rule 4: *say what the design says, where it says it, once*), and Apple's VoiceOver practice.

1. **The tense of the house is "already".** The Feed says *I already was*; the Light says *I already am*; the Point says *I already knew* (canon, ledger 268). A string never frames a thing as pending, owed or unlocked. Words like *yet*, *not yet*, *soon*, *later in*, *unlock* and *progress* are banned in chrome. The one exception is *not yet* as an answer **he** gives (the wager). *Why:* "nothing hidden-until" is a grammar, not a mechanic. *Yet* is the hidden-until of language.
2. **Person is fixed by who speaks.**
   - The house's chrome speaks to **you**.
   - A body's story speaks as **I**; his memo speaks of **he**.
   - The dot (Bindu, Lalita) speaks as **I** (*found me*, *…that was me, playing.*).
   - His own words, quoted back, are **I** (*I carry …*, *I'll carry this*, *the next time I call*).
   - People are roles (*the one who…*), never names.
   - The chrome never says *he* to him.

   *Why:* person is how the reader knows who is in the room.
3. **No mechanism words, in any channel.** This covers visible text, VoiceOver labels, hints, values, custom actions, rotors and announcements. The full banned list is in §5.3. *Why:* GAMEPLAN law 10. VoiceOver is a screen too, and in the scores it is where most of the breaches hide.
4. **Nothing counted, in any channel.** No numeral about him or a story. No "n of N", no VoiceOver value, no percentage. Dates are names and stay. Age is TimeWords. Roman numerals stay only where the design authored them. *Why:* law 1. A count spoken aloud is still a count.
5. **Invitation, never instruction.** Chrome may say what a thing *is* or *offer* a door: `one line is enough ›`, `let a name surface ›`. It does not teach. An instruction exists only in three forms:
   - authored by the design (the 18 world cues);
   - registered `APP-OWN-INSTRUCTIONAL`, said once, and withdrawn once learned (W3's GuideVoice);
   - shown only while the finger is down (`hold to open it now`).

   No legend ever explains a layout. *Why:* Rule 4, as CLAUDE §10 restated it. The Lalita test fails on explanation.
6. **Silence is complete.** Missing is absent (rupakarshini law 22). No surface says it is empty, waiting or still loading in apologetic words. A whole screen that has nothing states what *is*, in the present, or shows only its figure. *Why:* shabdakarshini: *"silence treated as complete — not apologetic, not a gap."*
7. **One name per thing, one thing per name.** A thing keeps the same word in every register and in VoiceOver. A word is never borrowed for a second thing: two *Books*, two *quiet rooms* and three *Turns* are breaches. *Why:* he learns the house by its words. Doubled words are doubled doors.
8. **"Beside" is the house's word for rhyme.** Rhyme is shown as threads and never named. Wherever words must carry it — VoiceOver, a chip, the Present — the word is *beside*: `, beside <title>` · `beside · <title>` · `lay it beside…`. *Why:* one image, used every time, becomes the name without being a name.
9. **The verbs of the house are few and keep one meaning each.**
   - *touch* — the hand (never *tap* in new copy).
   - *hold* — a timed press.
   - *keep* — hold on to.
   - *carry* — take with you.
   - *let go* — release.
   - *seal* — close for later.
   - *receive* — take a story in.
   - *open* — go to.
   - *draw* — take one from many.
   - *lay down* — set something down at the Gates and in the Unwinding.

   *Why:* the verbs are the game (GAMEPLAN §3.3). A verb that means two things breaks a move.
10. **Register by typeface.**
    - Lora italic is a voice: his, a body's, the dot's, a question.
    - Lora upright is a story.
    - Space Mono uppercase is ceremonial chrome (`TOUCH TO READ`, `WHAT FOUND YOU`).
    - Space Mono lowercase is a quiet door or cue (`the voices ›`).
    - His own answers (`yes · a glimpse · not yet`, `offer it · hold in silence`, `I did it · let it be`) are his voice, so they are Lora italic, never mono.

    *Why:* the face tells him who is speaking before he reads a word.
11. **One punctuation.**
    - A spaced em dash for the turn (*Yesterday's story — how did it sit?*).
    - ` · ` joins pairs.
    - `›` is a door forward; `‹` is the way out.
    - `…` (one character) is reserved for things arriving.
    - Quotation marks are curly.
    - No exclamation marks anywhere. A question mark appears only in asks.
    - British spelling, as the canon already writes it (*colour*, *centre*).

    *Why:* shabdakarshini Universal Rules 1–4. The canon already uses *name, colour, mark* and *TO THE CENTRE*.
12. **The canon is not paraphrased; its slot is part of it.** An authored line is never reworded or shortened, and it is never moved into a second slot where it would mean something else (CLAUDE §10, *wrong slot*). A new surface gets new words; it does not borrow a ceremony's line. *Why:* the Rite's `received at the pace of breath` belongs to the Rite.
13. **VoiceOver speaks in the house's voice, by one grammar.**
    - **Label** = what it is, as a lowercase noun phrase in house words.
    - **Action** = his verb, lowercase.
    - **Hint** = only where the destination is not obvious. Six words at most. Never instructional ("double-tap to…"). Never "button".
    - **Value** = only `holding` / `held`.
    - A glyph-only control always carries a label; VoiceOver must never read "single left-pointing angle quotation mark".

    *Why:* law 7 (an accessibility twin for every move) with no voice of its own would be read as Apple's voice, not the house's.
14. **Profoundly simple, simply profound.**
    - Chrome lines run 2–7 words. Subs run 2–6 words. Asks are one sentence.
    - No two lines on one screen say the same thing.
    - The "already" sentences (§5.4) each have one home.

    *Why:* shabdakarshini's governing constraint for perspective writing. When in doubt, err quieter.

- The Turn list goes from 12 rows to **24** (W0 +3, W3 +2, W4 +4, W6 +3). No score lists the final rows with their subs, so no one checks that 24 subs are 24 different images. Three are near-duplicates today:

### 3.5 VoiceOver: designed per wave, never as one voice
Each score writes its own VoiceOver strings. Taken together they hold **six grammars**:
- nouns (`the cloth`);
- imperatives (`turn the cloth over`);
- questions;
- glyph names (none, so Apple's);
- hints that teach (`opens beside this story` is fine; `<voice>, wordless — touch to hear` is fine, though it is the only hint that instructs);
- labels that state mechanism.

No score labels the **back chevron, the hub mark, the Ash mark, the ▲ resonance, the ∞ seek or the room glyphs on the ring outside W0/W2**. Under VoiceOver the house opens with Apple reading Unicode names.
