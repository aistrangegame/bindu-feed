The Reading

five worlds · five ways of reading one star · comp 4

D5.1, rendered. The four sections — SAY · WALK · HAND · OPEN — are the same four in every world. What differs is how they arrive, and the arriving is the world.

The seven worlds are not seven ways of picking a star. They are seven ways of reading one.

The five gestures

I · stillnessCome to rest near a star and stay. Nothing opens by being acted on — the sections surface out of the white. Move and it closes, without complaint and without penalty. The inversion: the reading displaces the field, A = p·(1−given/4·0.62) — and the stack carries itself upward as it grows, so by the fourth the reading has taken the field and the star's own light is nearly all that is left.
II · followingTake hold of a ray and travel out along it. SAY near the origin, OPEN at the rim — reading a star is the movement from One to many, performed. The hand-off is exclusive: this is one reading along a continuum, so the section behind you goes dark as the next arrives. Stop and the arm reels you gently back. Spanda pulses down all nine arms on the breath clock; the light is white at the centre and separates into its hue further out. The followed arm brightens and slows; the other eight dim and hurry on.
III · partingPress and hold: a parting opens where your hand is, and every section must be read through a parting you are actively holding. Let go and it closes behind you — you never lift the veil, you only part it. But each section received leaves that zone permanently thinner: the veil remembers what was handed back and does not take it again.
IV · bearingLight from below — you are the magma layer. The vault descends on the breath and the walls bow inward. The reading is letterpress: press back, and the harder the press the deeper the strike. Below 0.12 there is no impression at all. Release and the stone relaxes — and what was struck stays struck.
VII · motionThe marketplace does not wait. Catch a star and keep pace: the frame turns with it while the others keep whirling, and the four sections fly in and land one at a time as you hold — caught in flight, never stacked; each lands as the one before it flies on. Let go early and the rest scatters — not a punishment, the tenth ox-herding picture.

⚠ Two things I could not source

I · the Laboratories' placement. The design says they "sit far out at the edge, arriving by their own road" but the coordinate lines fall outside what I read. Mine are approximate and marked in code — do not treat them as canon.

IV · what "harder" means on a mouse. The design says the harder the press, the deeper the inscription. Touch and pencil report real pressure; a mouse reports none, so the fallback is sustained hold. Both feed one press value, shown live on the right gauge. On device, use the real thing.
